Group discussion days

Clusters 2 and 3 each have a GD day the day before. A firm that holds a GD runs one GD process for all its roles: it screens everyone who applied to any of those roles once, and calls twice as many students as its roles' interview shortlists would add up to (at least one full group). The GD is the first cut: a student not called to the GD cannot be interviewed by that firm. After the GD each role draws its hotlist and shortlist from GD attendees who applied to it, and if it runs short on the day it extends only to other GD attendees, best GD performance first. Attendance is compulsory and a student can be invited to any number of GDs, so no student may be in two GDs at once. GDs are held in the two syndicate rooms either side of the Placecom desk at each wing entrance (18 rooms). A company runs its GDs as one block in one wing with one or two panels, because its Placecom volunteer stays in that wing, and a room passes to the next company only after a one-hour gap (editable). No company starts its GDs after 6 pm, and the optimiser pulls the most popular companies to the start of the day (both editable on the Assumptions tab). After the GD each firm is one step more confident: defensive becomes competing, competing becomes confident, and confident cuts its list from 2X to 1.5X. Firms without a GD go straight to their hotlist and shortlist, and extend to any remaining applicant.

Planning × matching (2×2 experiment)

Runs all four combinations of baseline or optimised pre-day planning with baseline or optimised live matching on the same seeds, with cohort allocation and GD timetables held fixed. Each seed is a pair across the four cells, so effects are paired differences with 95% t intervals. Event-keyed random numbers give every cell the same luck for the same event; the legacy stream reproduces the report's numbers. Runs in a background worker; large experiments are better run with tools/experiment.js and imported here.

Cohorts and clusters

Each company sits in one cohort by its core business, and the whole cohort comes in one cluster. Move cohorts between clusters to test non-optimal allocations.

Solve the allocation as a 0-1 programme (branch and bound)

Each cohort goes to exactly one cluster. The objective rewards putting preferred cohorts earlier, weighted by their number of roles: the sum over cohorts of (4 − cluster) × average popularity × roles. Caps keep each cluster within the rooms available. Every LP relaxation is solved by the simplex method; nodes are explored depth-first, taking the y = 1 branch first, and fathomed by bound, by integrality or by infeasibility.
